## Thesis Summary

**Forgent is one of the purest public plays on the 'grid-to-chip' electrical bottleneck of the AI buildout — a U.S. designer/manufacturer of automatic transfer switches, switchgear, transformers, PDUs, power skids, and prefabricated eHouses that connect utility power to hyperscale data center loads.** It is a private-equity roll-up (Neos Partners combined four legacy electrical-equipment companies) that IPO'd in February 2026 and is growing explosively: fiscal Q3 2026 revenue +103% YoY to $379M, a record ~$1.98B backlog (+157% YoY), a 2.3x book-to-bill, and raised FY26 guidance to $1.35–1.39B revenue and $310–320M adjusted EBITDA.

**The central risks are structural rather than operational:** a controlled-company/dual-class Up-C governance structure with a tax receivable agreement that channels value to the PE sponsor, a public float that exists mainly to provide sponsor liquidity (not growth capital), a premium valuation (~7–8x trailing revenue), and a very short public track record.

## 1. Company Information

### Business Overview

Forgent Power Solutions (NYSE: FPS), headquartered in Dayton, Minnesota, designs and manufactures electrical distribution ('powertrain') equipment for three end markets: data centers, the electric power grid, and energy-intensive industrial facilities. Its catalog spans automatic transfer switches (ATS), low- and medium-voltage switchgear, low-voltage and padmount and VPI transformers, panelboards, paralleling switchgear, PDU transformers, power distribution units, power skids, generator connection cabinets, remote power panels, substation transformers, and prefabricated eHouses/gear. A majority of revenue is custom-engineered for specific projects with significant engineering consultation — the differentiator management emphasizes, alongside among the shortest lead times in the industry.

Forgent occupies the same physical-infrastructure layer of the AI stack as Vertiv, but is narrower and further upstream toward the utility interconnect: where Vertiv leans into power/cooling inside the data hall (including liquid cooling), Forgent concentrates on the medium/low-voltage electrical distribution that steps grid power down into the facility. Data centers were ~42% of FY2025 revenue and ~47% of backlog — making FPS one of the most concentrated listed vehicles for the electrical-bottleneck theme, with the grid and industrial businesses providing a second growth leg from electrification and grid upgrades.

**Origin:** Forgent was formed in 2023 by San Diego-based private equity firm Neos Partners, LP, which acquired and combined four legacy electrical-equipment businesses into a single platform. It is a young corporate entity (roll-up integration risk) operating in a very old, established product category.

### Leadership

**CEO: Gary Niederpruem —** Previously Chief Strategy & Marketing Officer at Vertiv, and held senior roles at Emerson Network Power (VP Global Marketing / GM Integrated Solutions) and Danaher. His Vertiv/Emerson pedigree is directly relevant: it is the same lineage that built the modern data center physical-infrastructure playbook, and it informs Forgent's custom-solutions, short-lead-time positioning.

**CFO: Ryan Fiedler —** Joined August 2025 to lead financial strategy, planning, and reporting ahead of the IPO. A short-tenured finance team plus a first-year public reporting cadence is worth weighing when assessing guidance reliability.

**Control:** Neos Partners retains majority voting control via a dual-class structure. This is a controlled company — public holders have limited influence over governance, capital allocation, and strategy.

### Total Addressable Market (TAM)

- **North American T&D:** ~$116B (2025), growing modestly at the aggregate level but with switchgear a >26% share segment growing ~7%+ CAGR through 2035.
- **North American industrial electrical components:** ~$112B (2025) projected toward ~$187B by 2032 (~7.5% CAGR).
- **Demand driver:** Data center construction is the accelerant — the constrained, long-lead-time electrical equipment (transformers, switchgear) is a recognized bottleneck in bringing AI capacity online, which is why Forgent's backlog is growing far faster than the underlying market CAGR (share gains + share of a surging data center sub-segment).

### Market Position & Competition

Forgent competes against far larger, diversified electrical-equipment incumbents. Its edge is agility: custom engineering, fast lead times, and a data-center-first orientation — but it is a small, single-platform challenger in a category dominated by global industrials with deep balance sheets and manufacturing scale.

| Company | Position / Notes |

| Eaton (ETN) | Diversified global power-management leader; in-house breakers/switchgear; large data-center franchise. Scale and supply-chain depth Forgent cannot match. |
| Vertiv (VRT) | Data center physical infrastructure leader (power + cooling); overlapping data-center customer base; Forgent's CEO is ex-Vertiv. |
| Schneider, ABB, Siemens Energy | Global T&D/switchgear/transformer incumbents with vast capacity and installed base. |

**Forgent's competitive advantages:** (1) Custom-engineered, short-lead-time delivery into a supply-constrained category where time-to-power is the binding constraint on data center commissioning; (2) data-center-first focus and a leadership team with Vertiv/Emerson DNA; (3) a ~$1.98B backlog and 2.3x book-to-bill giving revenue visibility well into FY27; (4) capacity expansion toward ~$5B revenue support. The mirror-image caveats: it is sub-scale versus Eaton/Schneider/ABB, unproven as a public company, and its 'moat' is execution/lead-time rather than a structural technology barrier.

### Balance Sheet & Cash Flow — Watch Items

- **GAAP vs. adjusted gap:** Trailing GAAP EPS is small (~$0.07–0.08) while adjusted metrics are far larger. The gap reflects roll-up/purchase accounting, IPO/secondary costs, TRA effects, and stock comp — scrutinize the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge before underwriting the adjusted figures.
- **IPO proceeds did not fund growth:** Forgent's ~$428M primary proceeds were used to redeem Neos-controlled operating-subsidiary interests, not for capex or the balance sheet. Growth capacity is being funded from operations/existing structure.
- **Transition to cash generation:** Management flags a 'transition toward cash generation' — i.e., FCF was not the story early on as it built capacity and working capital for a 100%+ growth ramp. Confirm working-capital intensity as backlog converts.
- **Tax Receivable Agreement (TRA):** Forgent will pay 85% of realized tax savings to pre-IPO owners; the S-1 notes TRA payments may in some cases be accelerated and exceed actual benefits — a real cash claim on future earnings that accrues to the sponsor, not public holders.

### Valuation

At ~$10–11.5B market cap on ~$1.35–1.39B FY26 revenue, FPS trades around ~7–8x trailing/forward revenue — a premium to diversified electrical-equipment peers (Eaton, Powell) that reflects its pure-play data-center exposure and ~80–100% growth. On EBITDA, ~$315M FY26 adjusted EBITDA against the market cap implies a rich EV/EBITDA multiple that only makes sense if the ~$5B revenue-capacity build converts and margins expand as guided. The analyst range ($51–$73 targets) is bullish, but the stock's own $26–$66 post-IPO range shows how violently the market re-rates this name on each print and secondary. This is a growth-and-execution valuation with essentially no margin of safety on a normalized-earnings basis.

## 4. Investment Thesis: Bull vs. Bear

### Bull Case

**A pure-play on the most constrained layer of AI infrastructure, growing ~100% with visibility.** Time-to-power is the binding constraint on bringing AI data center capacity online, and the electrical distribution equipment Forgent makes is exactly what is in short supply. Forgent pairs that demand with custom engineering and among the shortest lead times in the industry, and it is converting it into a ~$1.98B backlog and a 2.3x book-to-bill — visibility a young company rarely has.

- **Explosive, accelerating growth:** Revenue +103% YoY with YoY growth higher in Q3 than Q2 despite a larger base.
- **Backlog + book-to-bill:** ~$1.98B backlog (+157%), 2.3x book-to-bill, 'fully booked' against Q4 — near-term revenue de-risked.
- **Margin expansion runway:** ~22.4% adjusted EBITDA margin with startup costs still a drag; operating leverage as ~$5B capacity fills.
- **Guidance raised across the board:** New low end above prior high end; adjusted net income guide +~128%.
- **Rare pure-play + credible team:** Vertiv/Emerson-pedigree leadership; one of the few listed ways to isolate the electrical-bottleneck theme.
- **Analyst support:** Strong Buy consensus, ~$60 mean target vs. ~$37 price.

### Bear Case

**The risks are structural and governance-driven, not demand-driven.** This is a PE-controlled roll-up that went public primarily to give its sponsor liquidity: the IPO and secondaries are largely Neos selling, primary proceeds were used to redeem sponsor interests rather than fund growth, and a Tax Receivable Agreement channels 85% of tax savings to pre-IPO owners. Public shareholders hold a minority-vote, economically disadvantaged position in a company with a one-quarter-old public track record and a rich valuation.

- **Controlled-company / dual-class Up-C:** Neos retains majority voting control; limited public-holder influence over governance and capital allocation.
- **TRA drag:** 85% of tax savings paid to pre-IPO owners; possible acceleration exceeding actual benefits — a structural leak of value.
- **IPO as liquidity event:** No growth capital retained; deal structure prioritizes sponsor exit. Recurring secondaries create persistent supply overhang.
- **Premium valuation, thin GAAP earnings:** ~7–8x revenue and rich EV/EBITDA vs. diversified peers; trailing GAAP EPS ~$0.07–0.08. Adjusted-heavy reporting requires scrutiny.
- **Roll-up integration risk:** Four legacy companies combined since 2023; integration, systems, and culture are unproven at this growth rate.
- **Concentration & cyclicality:** Heavy data-center exposure ties the story to hyperscaler capex; any AI-capex deceleration hits backlog conversion. Sub-scale vs. Eaton/Schneider/ABB.
- **Extreme volatility:** Post-IPO range $26–$66; the market re-rates violently on each print and offering.

### Balanced Read

Forgent is the most operationally exciting and the most structurally encumbered of the AI-infrastructure names in this series. Operationally it looks like an early-innings Vertiv analog — same customer wave, same time-to-power tailwind, faster growth off a tiny base. But unlike Vertiv (seasoned public company), Micron (cycle debate), or Marvell (customer-retention debate), Forgent's core question is 'who does the value accrue to, and can I trust one quarter of public numbers?' The demand and backlog are real; the governance, TRA, sponsor-selling, and valuation mean public shareholders may not capture the full economics even if the business performs. This is a higher-risk, story-stock position where the diligence burden is on the deal structure and the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge as much as on end-market demand.

## 5. Summary & Verdict

**Forgent is a genuine pure-play on the electrical bottleneck of the AI buildout, with best-in-class growth and unusually strong backlog visibility for a company its age — wrapped in a private-equity control structure that tilts economics toward the sponsor.** The operating story is excellent: +103% revenue, ~$1.98B backlog, 2.3x book-to-bill, guidance raised with the new floor above the old ceiling, and a leadership team carrying the Vertiv/Emerson data-center playbook. If the ~$5B capacity build converts and margins expand as startup costs roll off, this looks like an early-stage Vertiv analog.

**The reasons for caution are not about whether the business is growing — it clearly is — but about structure, disclosure maturity, and price.** A controlled-company dual-class Up-C, an 85% tax receivable agreement, an IPO whose proceeds funded a sponsor redemption rather than growth, recurring Neos secondaries, a one-quarter public track record, and a ~7–8x revenue multiple against thin GAAP earnings together mean public holders bear real structural and valuation risk. This is a story stock where the deal mechanics deserve as much diligence as the end market.

### Position Sizing Considerations

- Treat as a high-risk, high-volatility small position (recent IPO, ~$26–$66 range, thin public history).
- Same macro bet — AI/data-center capex — as Vertiv, Micron, and Marvell; it is not independent diversification but another correlated slice of the same theme, and the closest overlap is with Vertiv specifically.
- Consider waiting for 1–2 more public quarters and the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge before sizing up; the Sept ~2 print is the next data point.
- Secondary offerings and lock-up expiries are known supply events — sizing into them is an event bet.
