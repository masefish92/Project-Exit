## Thesis Summary

**Micron is the only U.S.-listed pure-play on the AI memory supercycle — a structural DRAM/NAND oligopoly (Micron, SK Hynix, Samsung control 90%+ of DRAM) experiencing the tightest supply/demand imbalance in its history.** The thesis rests on a genuine regime change: management states it can fulfill only 50–66% of medium-term customer demand, HBM and DRAM are contractually sold out through calendar 2027, and Micron has locked in ~$22B of Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs) that convert a historically boom-bust commodity model toward contracted, RPO-backed revenue. Fiscal Q3 2026 delivered $41.5B revenue (+167% YoY) at an 84.9% non-GAAP gross margin, with a Q4 guide of $50B and ~86% gross margin.

**The central bear argument is cyclicality and depreciation-driven mean reversion** (Michael Burry is short), not near-term demand — the debate is duration of the cycle, not whether the cycle exists.

## 1. Company Information

### Business Overview

Micron Technology, headquartered in Boise, Idaho, is one of three global memory manufacturers and the only one based in the United States. It designs and manufactures DRAM (its primary profit engine), NAND flash, and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) — the stacked-DRAM product that has become the binding constraint on AI accelerator performance. Micron is vertically integrated across design, fabrication, and advanced packaging, and sells into data center, mobile, client, automotive, and industrial end markets under the Micron and Crucial brands.

The company reorganized around AI end-demand into four segments: Cloud Memory Business Unit (CMBU — hyperscale/AI), Core Data Center Business Unit (CDBU), Mobile and Client Business Unit (MCBU), and Automotive and Embedded Business Unit (AEBU). In fiscal Q3 2026, CMBU alone generated $13.77B in revenue at an 83% gross margin — a segment that did not meaningfully exist at this scale two years ago.

Unlike the physical-infrastructure names (e.g. Vertiv, Schneider), Micron sits one layer deeper in the AI stack: it is a picks-and-shovels supplier whose product is consumed inside every GPU server. HBM content per accelerator rises with each NVIDIA and AMD generation, giving Micron unit-and-price tailwinds simultaneously — the defining feature of the current cycle.

### Leadership

**Chairman, President & CEO: Sanjay Mehrotra —** Co-founder of SanDisk and Micron CEO since 2017. Has steered Micron through the 2019 and 2023 memory downturns and repositioned the company as an HBM contender. Frames AI as a permanent structural shift in memory economics rather than a cyclical spike, and has emphasized the SCA model as a business-model transformation toward durability and predictability.

**CFO: Mark Murphy —** Oversees the capital-allocation framework prioritizing R&D and capex, disciplined debt reduction (net cash position at record levels), and buybacks constrained by CHIPS Act grant conditions (federal grants legally limit large-scale repurchases, so 'total return' is delivered via debt paydown and dividends).

### Total Addressable Market (TAM)

- **HBM:** ~$35B (2025) projected toward $100B+ by 2027–2028 (~40% CAGR); Micron management projects HBM TAM to exceed $100B in 2027.
- **Overall DRAM:** Grew ~260% YoY and ~80% QoQ in Q1 2026 per Counterpoint; a three-player oligopoly (>90% share).
- **Data-center memory & enterprise SSD:** Micron data-center revenue >$25B/quarter; enterprise SSD ~$5B (20% of data-center revenue), more than doubling sequentially.
- **Structural driver:** Rising memory intensity per AI server (HBM stacks, higher-capacity DRAM for agentic AI/CPU control planes), plus supply that management and peers say remains below demand 'for the foreseeable future.'

### Market Position & Competition

Memory is a three-firm oligopoly. In overall DRAM (Q2 2026, Counterpoint), Samsung led at ~39%, SK Hynix ~26%, and Micron ~25% — Micron has closed to within ~1 point of SK Hynix for the #2 spot. In HBM specifically, SK Hynix remains the leader (~50–62%), holding roughly two-thirds of NVIDIA's HBM4 (Rubin) allocation; Micron holds a smaller but rapidly growing share and has reached HBM4 12-high yields faster than it did HBM3E.

| Company | Position / Notes |

**Micron's competitive advantages:** (1) Only U.S.-domiciled memory maker — CHIPS Act grants (up to $6.4B) and >$200B announced U.S. investment give supply-chain and policy insulation; (2) fastest HBM4 yield ramp positions it to take NVIDIA/AMD allocation share into the Rubin cycle; (3) leadership in data-center DRAM and enterprise SSD (>$5B, doubling sequentially); (4) SCA model converts commodity exposure into contracted RPO; (5) record net-cash balance sheet funds a >$27B capex program peers with weaker balance sheets cannot match.

### The Structural Story — SCAs, Sold-Out Supply, RPO

The single most important development is the Strategic Customer Agreement framework. Micron has entered 16 SCAs representing ~$22B in total cash and financial commitments (~$18B cash), and — for agreements with defined or floor/ceiling pricing — now discloses remaining performance obligations (RPO). HBM3E and HBM4 are fully booked through calendar 2027 with demand extending into 2028; DRAM and NAND 2027 output is reportedly sold out across all three suppliers, with buyers pre-depositing to secure allocation and getting only ~60–70% of requested volume. This is the mechanism the bull case uses to argue 'this cycle is different' — contracted, pre-funded, multi-year visibility that memory has historically never had.

**Read-through:** Micron is self-funding an enormous capacity build from operating cash flow while still cutting debt and raising the dividend — a first-order change from prior cycles when capex was debt-financed into gluts. ### Valuation

Micron screens 'cheap' on trailing metrics (~20x TTM P/E) precisely because earnings are near a cyclical peak — the classic memory value trap where low P/E marks the top, not a bargain. On forward peak EPS (UBS models ~$265 EPS by 2028; some estimates imply low-single-digit forward P/E on peak numbers), the multiple looks extraordinarily low, but that is only defensible if the cycle's duration is genuinely extended by the SCA/sold-out dynamics. The honest framing: valuation is entirely a function of one's view on cycle duration and mid-cycle normalized earnings, not on the current multiple.

## 4. Investment Thesis: Bull vs. Bear

### Bull Case

**'This time is different' — structural, not cyclical.** The bull case argues the SCA framework, sold-out-through-2027 supply, and pre-funded customer deposits convert memory from a boom-bust commodity into a contracted, RPO-backed franchise with unprecedented visibility. HBM demand grows with every GPU generation (memory bandwidth is the binding constraint on AI performance), and the three-player oligopoly has shown capital discipline rather than the reckless overbuilding of past cycles.

- **Records everywhere:** $41.5B revenue, 84.9% GM, $25.11 EPS in Q3; $50B / ~86% / $31 EPS Q4 guide.
- **Visibility:** HBM sold out through CY2027, demand into 2028; ~$22B SCA commitments.
- **Balance sheet:** $30.2B cash, record net cash, self-funding >$27B capex while cutting debt and raising the dividend.
- **Share gains:** Closing on SK Hynix for #2 DRAM; fastest HBM4 yield ramp; U.S./CHIPS Act moat.
- **Cheap on peak EPS:** If the cycle is extended, low-single-digit to ~5x forward P/E on 2027–2028 numbers is deeply undervalued.

### Bear Case

**Michael Burry's framing — 'Micron defines cyclical like no other.'** Burry cites 34 drawdowns of >30% over 42 years, a median ROIC of ~4% and ROE ~7% ('frankly terrible'), free cash flow negative ~48% of the time, and 'one quarter in three, Micron is a destroyer of capital.' He argues the rally reflects FOMO and greater-fool dynamics around 'sold out through 2026,' and that the stock was more extended above its 200-day moving average than at any point since 1984. He has separately flagged a ~$176B hyperscaler depreciation gap (2026–2028): if data-center customers are stretching useful-life schedules to inflate earnings, real end-demand and future memory orders may be weaker than reported.

- **Peak-cycle earnings:** Low trailing P/E marks a top; mid-cycle normalized EPS is a fraction of peak. Margins mean-revert violently when supply catches up.
- **Depreciation wall:** >$27B FY26 capex rising in FY27 builds a large future depreciation base that crushes margins in the next downcycle.
- **Pricing already wobbling:** HBM3E prices fell on renegotiation in Q2 2026; HBM4 launch delays hit even SK Hynix — a reminder pricing is not one-directional.
- **China/CXMT:** ~716% YoY growth to ~7% DRAM share; Apple testing CXMT — legacy-node commoditization is a structural long-term risk.
- **Circular-demand concern:** Some AI infrastructure demand may be supplier-funded/circular, blurring true end-demand.
- **Volatility:** Beta ~3.0; a ~28% drawdown occurred in July 2026 alone. Not a low-risk holding.

### Balanced Read

Both sides are partly right and the disagreement is almost entirely about time horizon. Near-term (through FY2027), the bull case has the facts: contracted, sold-out, pre-funded supply makes a sudden demand air-pocket unlikely, and Burry's 'sold out through 2026' line is already stale (it's now 2027, extending to 2028). Longer-term, the bear case is structurally sound: memory has always mean-reverted, the depreciation base being built is enormous, and Chinese supply is the eventual pressure valve. The question is not 'cyclical or not' — Micron is cyclical — but whether SCAs genuinely lengthen this cycle enough to earn multiple years of peak cash flow before normalization. Position sizing and time horizon matter more here than the point estimate of fair value.

## 5. Summary & Verdict

**Micron is the highest-beta, highest-conviction way to express a view on the durability of the AI memory cycle.** The fundamental facts are exceptional and not in dispute: record revenue and margins, a $50B quarterly guide, sold-out supply through 2027, ~$22B of contracted SCAs, and a record net-cash balance sheet self-funding a >$27B capex program. The transformation from commodity cyclical toward contracted supplier — if it holds — is the most important structural change in Micron's history.

**The bear case is not about the present; it is about mean reversion.** Burry's short is a bet on cycle duration and the depreciation math, not on Q4 numbers. He may well be early or wrong on timing — 'sold out through 2026' has already extended to 2028 — but the structural cyclicality he describes is real and has repeated 34 times in 42 years. The risk is not that the business breaks; it is that the multiple and earnings both normalize from a peak, which for a beta-3.0 stock can mean a 40–60% drawdown even inside a secular uptrend.

### Position Sizing Considerations

- Best suited to investors with high risk tolerance and a defined view on cycle duration and mid-cycle EPS.
- The July 2026 ~28% drawdown illustrates the volatility; scaling in on pullbacks is more defensible than chasing near highs.
- The binary risk is cyclical timing (when does peak roll over), not business-model failure.
- Given beta ~3.0, size smaller than a typical core holding; consider it a high-volatility satellite AI-infrastructure position.
