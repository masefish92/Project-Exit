Thesis Summary

Marvell is the #2 player in the custom AI-silicon (XPU/ASIC) duopoly and a leader in AI optical interconnect — the two arms-dealer businesses that capture hyperscaler capex regardless of which GPU or accelerator wins. Together with Broadcom it controls ~90–95% of the custom AI ASIC co-design market; Marvell holds ~20–25% share, anchored by AWS Trainium and Microsoft Maia. The data center segment is now ~76% of revenue and growing ~50%+, with management guiding FY2027 revenue to ~$11.5B (+40%) and FY2028 to ~$16.5B, and citing line-of-sight to >$10B in annual custom silicon revenue by FY2029.

The central bear argument is a combination of extreme customer concentration (a handful of hyperscaler programs), a well-capitalized Broadcom that keeps winning the marquee sockets, and a valuation that already prices multi-year flawless execution — crystallized by reports that AWS may move Trainium 3/4 to Alchip.

What Changed / Why Now

  • Q1 FY2027 (reported May 27, 2026): Record revenue $2.418B (+28% YoY, +9% QoQ); data center $1.83B (+27% YoY, 76% of mix); non-GAAP EPS $0.80; non-GAAP operating margin ~35%.
  • Fourth straight multi-billion-dollar guidance raise: FY2027 lifted to ~$11.5B (+$5B vs. prior view, ~+40% YoY); FY2028 raised to ~$16.5B (+$1.5B in one quarter). Interconnect now seen growing >70% in FY27 (up from 50%).
  • NVIDIA partnership + $2B investment (late March 2026): Expanded across NVLink Fusion, silicon photonics, and AI RAN — positions Marvell inside both the GPU and ASIC ecosystems.
  • M&A to extend the optical/photonics roadmap: Closed Celestial AI (photonic fabric), Polariton (plasmonic silicon photonics, >1 THz bandwidth), and XConn — pushing toward 3.2T and beyond.
  • The overhang: Benchmark downgraded to Hold on reports AWS could shift Trainium 3/4 designs to Alchip; the stock fell sharply from its ~$329 record high (a >30% drawdown into mid-2026) before an August rebound. Customer-concentration risk is now the defining debate.
