Thesis Summary

Micron is the only U.S.-listed pure-play on the AI memory supercycle — a structural DRAM/NAND oligopoly (Micron, SK Hynix, Samsung control 90%+ of DRAM) experiencing the tightest supply/demand imbalance in its history. The thesis rests on a genuine regime change: management states it can fulfill only 50–66% of medium-term customer demand, HBM and DRAM are contractually sold out through calendar 2027, and Micron has locked in ~$22B of Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs) that convert a historically boom-bust commodity model toward contracted, RPO-backed revenue. Fiscal Q3 2026 delivered $41.5B revenue (+167% YoY) at an 84.9% non-GAAP gross margin, with a Q4 guide of $50B and ~86% gross margin.

The central bear argument is cyclicality and depreciation-driven mean reversion (Michael Burry is short), not near-term demand — the debate is duration of the cycle, not whether the cycle exists.

What Changed / Why Now

  • Q3 FY2026 blowout (June 24, 2026): Revenue $41.46B vs. ~$35.7B consensus (+16% beat); non-GAAP EPS $25.11 vs. ~$20.5 est.; gross margin 84.9%; data-center revenue exceeded $25B (>$100B annualized run-rate).
  • Q4 FY2026 guide of $50B ±$1B, ~86% gross margin, EPS $31.00 ±$1.00 — a guide that alone exceeds several full prior fiscal years of revenue.
  • Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs): 16 agreements, ~$22B total cash and financial commitments (~$18B cash), now disclosed as remaining performance obligations (RPO) — the structural story that reframes Micron from commodity cyclical toward contracted supplier.
  • Balance sheet inflection: Q3 ended with $30.2B cash/investments; net cash at strongest level ever after >$5B debt reduction over three quarters; 30% dividend increase to $0.15/quarter.
  • The bear got louder: Michael Burry disclosed and increased a MU short into ~$900–1,000, calling memory the archetypal capital-destroying cyclical and flagging a hyperscaler depreciation gap. A sharp ~28% July drawdown followed, then an August rebound.
